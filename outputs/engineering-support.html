What this service covers

Technical review

Drawings, equipment data, site conditions, vendor proposals, and the assumptions holding the design together.

Decision support

Options side by side, the tradeoff that actually decides it, and a recommended action.

Documentation

Review notes, issue logs, and summaries a non-technical stakeholder can read without translation.

Useful when

  • Two engineers disagree and the project needs a tiebreaker.
  • An owner is about to approve a proposal nobody has independently checked.
  • A facility issue keeps coming back after each repair.
  • A technical problem has to be explained to a board, a lender, or a tenant.

What you receive

  • Technical review memo
  • Options and tradeoff summary
  • Issue and risk register
  • Recommended next-step plan

Engagement process

Define the decision

What has to be decided, by when, and what information already exists.

Review the evidence

Technical data, operating constraints, and the risk each option carries.

Recommend next steps

A written recommendation, the assumptions behind it, and what is still missing.

A good first message

“We are replacing a chilled water pump skid. The vendor’s proposal assumes the existing header can carry the new flow, and our mechanical contractor believes it cannot. Nobody has put numbers to it.

We need this settled before the purchase order goes out on 30 September. I can send the submittal, the original piping drawings, and last summer’s pressure readings.”

Four sentences is plenty: the technical question, why it is stuck, when it matters, and what you can share. If you have less than this, send it anyway.
